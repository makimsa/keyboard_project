# Project Proposal
For the proposal, we want to have:

- A written description of what you hope to accomplish
- Links to resources you’ve found so far
- Parts that you’re considering using. If you are planning to use some major parts from around the lab, this might be a good time to mention them to ensure you’re not going to conflict with another group.
- A high-level block diagram of how your parts will fit together
- What you would expect to accomplish to earn an ‘A’ on the project. Consider listing out a minimum set of features you're planning to implement, plus any "stretch goals". We’ll work with you to adjust these (if needed) so that each team has realistic but challenging enough goals.
- A hard deliverable for the mid-project review. Select one (or more) critically important parts of your project, and get it working.
- This gives us a chance to help you re-target your project if you hit a big roadblock.

>Note: You are required to discuss your project proposal in person with your instructor or TA.  You will receive a 0 for your proposal and not have an approved project if you do not discuss it in person. 

The proposal doesn’t need to be long - one to three pages of total length is typical. The more of your project you have planned at this stage, the more accurate your assessment of the issues involved will be. Feel free to give us all the details of your research. However, ensure you have all aspects described above in your write-up to receive full points.

On the day the proposals are turned in, we’ll work with each group to provide feedback and tweak your proposal until we’re happy with what you’re proposing. We will also try to give feedback before class for any proposals that are turned in early (you can resubmit as many times as you want). If we think your proposal is too easy, we’ll ask how you can make it more interesting. If your project is too hard, we’ll ask how you can pare it down to make it manageable.
